A Short History of the Printing Press

Before the fifteenth century, books in Europe were copied by hand, which made them slow to produce and expensive to own. Around 1440, Johannes Gutenberg introduced a printing press in Mainz that combined movable metal type, oil-based ink and a screw press adapted from those used for wine and olives.

The new technology spread quickly. Within fifty years, printing workshops had opened in hundreds of European towns, and millions of volumes were in circulation. Prices fell, literacy rose, and ideas moved faster than ever before. Printed texts helped standardize spelling and grammar, supported the growth of universities and played a part in the Reformation and the Scientific Revolution.

In later centuries, steam power, rotary presses and eventually digital printing continued to lower the cost of publishing. Historians often compare the changes brought by Gutenberg to those brought by the internet, since both made information far easier to copy and share.
